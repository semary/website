How I handle a product brief

We agree on the decision

Send the brief, prototype or proposal you already have. We agree on the question, who will use the answer and what the engagement will deliver.

I test it against the product

I trace the relevant journeys and business rules, build a prototype where it helps, and bring unresolved trade-offs back to you.

Your team gets a usable handoff

Depending on the brief, that could be a scoped backlog, a vendor comparison or a tested prototype with acceptance criteria.

Inside a payment review

A payment can look successful in one app while showing a different amount in the other. Here is how I check a simulated purchase before handoff.

Customer confirmation

Match the merchant and amount to the original request. Check the confirmation in Arabic and English.

Merchant receipt

Follow the payment reference to the merchant’s receipt. Both sides should identify the same transaction.

History and recovery

Check the activity entry and resulting balance. Interrupt the flow and see what each person is told when they retry.

Writing

Recent themes from my writing in Arabic and English. The links open my LinkedIn activity.

Egypt's next fintech wave is not another consumer app

Wallets, BNPL and bank apps are crowded. The opening is in the unglamorous layer underneath, reconciliation, risk engines, settlement, integrations. Apps get attention; systems create defensibility.

›

The $20 a month AI era might be temporary

A prompt, a long reasoning task and a generated video do not cost a provider the same thing. My read: chat gets cheaper, execution gets expensive, and pricing ends up closer to electricity than to Netflix.

›

Vision is not validation

A direction can feel right while the product, timing, and distribution remain unproven. Why those are separate questions.

›

From product manager to product executive

Why strong PMs stall around senior. At some point you stop being measured by what ships and start being measured by the quality of your judgement.

›

Most startup ideas do not fail because they are bad

A promising idea still needs clear assumptions, useful requirements, and a first release small enough to learn from.
